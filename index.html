About CPSL

One Incident Timeline From Four Separate Systems

At small UK industrial sites with 10 to 249 staff, a door controller, camera, machine and router can record parts of the same incident on different clocks and vocabularies, with no link between them. When equipment goes missing, a door is forced or an unknown device joins the network, reconstructing what happened by hand takes hours and is often abandoned.

CPSL is designed to show which events belong together across physical and digital systems, identify which sources were missing, and tell the site manager what to check first in plain English with a stated confidence. Camera footage stays on the site recorder, and nothing the business already owns needs replacing.

Each month, the service exports a dated monthly evidence pack recording incidents, actions taken and source coverage—ready to share with a customer, insurer or auditor. Headquartered in London, United Kingdom.

Core Technology

Five Core Elements & Edge Gateway

Designed for industrial sites with legacy equipment. Below is how the initial 12-week build relates to later roadmap work.

Initial Build (Weeks 1–12)

Twenty deterministic cross-domain rules reviewed with site managers, single-clock normalisation, gap marking and plain-English accounts.

Later Work (Months 8–18)

Site-learned statistical baselines and calibrated reconstruction confidence built on observed pilot and commercial site data.

Later Work (Months 19–30)

Learned reconstruction models trained on the labelled small-site incident corpus where telemetry evidence is missing.

Element A

Intelligence for Legacy Industrial Sites

Purpose-built cyber-physical intelligence for small warehouses, depots, workshops and supplier factories using existing equipment.

Unlike enterprise tools requiring mature asset discovery, OT transformation or continuous telemetry, CPSL works from the signals your cameras, door controllers, machines and routers already emit—even when a source is offline.

Initial build vs roadmap: The first version is planned around twenty deterministic cross-domain rules reviewed with site managers. Statistical baselines and calibrated confidence follow later (Months 8–18).

Element B

Cross-Domain Suspicion Chains

Links door access events, camera metadata, machine stoppages or logins and network activity into one ordered sequence.

Single-domain systems raise isolated alerts that look harmless on their own. CPSL links related events by time, place and order across physical and digital sources so a connected sequence is evaluated as one incident chain.

Initial build vs roadmap: Starts with twenty cross-domain rules in the 12-week build, expanding with statistical baselines and broader rule coverage on the later roadmap.

Element C

Reconstruction from Partial Evidence

Infers the most probable sequence when a step is missing, labels what was inferred and states reduced confidence.

When brownfield sensors drop offline, conventional tools fail silently. CPSL separates recorded observations from inferred steps so managers know both what was logged and where telemetry was missing.

Worked example — Illustrative workshop scenario (not a live customer incident)
  • 02:14ObservedThe rear door opens without a badge event.
  • 02:16ObservedAn unknown device joins the network.
  • 02:19ObservedThe diagnostic server records a port scan.
  • 02:21ObservedA controller records an unusual after-hours login.
  • GapInferredThe rear camera was offline for maintenance, so the system cannot identify who entered. CPSL labels the missing physical entry step as inferred and reports the chain at reduced confidence.

Roadmap: Calibrated confidence is planned for Months 8–18, with learned reconstruction models trained on labelled data following in Months 19–30.

Element D

Plain-English Operational Narratives

Turns linked cross-domain events into a concise account naming what happened, where, when and what is unusual.

Designed for site managers, owners and depot leads rather than security analysts. Every account lists which sources contributed, which were offline, and the confidence statement—avoiding dense charts and dashboard fatigue.

Incidents, manager actions and source coverage feed automatically into a dated monthly evidence pack for customers, insurers or auditors.

Element E

Mixed Physical & Digital Response Guidance

Recommends coordinated first physical and digital actions tied directly to the events in the chain.

Instead of separate IT playbooks or physical alarm escalations, CPSL gives the site manager clear first actions across both domains in order—and timestamps completed steps into the incident record.

In the illustrative workshop scenario, the first actions are to preserve controller logs, isolate the unknown device by its network address, check the rear door for damage and restore the offline camera.

Architecture

Edge Gateway & Existing Equipment

A commodity edge gateway connects via standard interfaces beside existing systems—raw footage stays on site.

The gateway reads camera metadata, tokenised door/badge events, machine fault/login logs and network syslog/flow records without replacing cameras or controllers. It sits beside site systems, never in front of them, so normal operations continue unaffected if the gateway goes offline.

A local store-and-forward buffer bridges broadband interruptions and backfills on reconnection. Event metadata, narratives and evidence packs are hosted in a UK cloud region with encryption in transit and at rest.

FAQ

Common Questions

Small warehouses, depots, workshops and supplier factories usually buy cameras, door access, connected machines and routers separately from different suppliers. Each logs on its own clock with no link to the others. CPSL pulls metadata and logs onto one reference clock, links related events across domains into a single suspicion chain, states what was observed versus inferred if a source was missing, and produces a plain-English narrative with first actions.
Enterprise platforms assume clean, continuous telemetry and a security analyst to read dashboards. CPSL is built for small brownfield sites where a camera or log may drop offline. When a step is unrecorded, the reconstruction layer identifies the most probable sequence, explicitly labels the inferred step and reports reduced confidence. The initial build uses twenty deterministic cross-domain rules, followed later by statistical baselines (Months 8–18) and learned reconstruction models (Months 19–30).
CPSL is designed to work with existing site equipment over standard interfaces so you do not have to replace hardware. Because older estates vary, a short remote site survey checks which of the four domains—cameras, door access, machines/controllers and network—are present and reachable before installation rather than promising compatibility with every brand upfront. The edge gateway sits beside existing systems, never in front of them, so site operations continue unchanged if the gateway goes offline.
Camera footage never leaves the site recorder. CPSL is designed to use metadata only—motion and object metadata, tokenised badge and asset identifiers (without employee names, photos or HR records), machine start/stop/fault/login events and network flow/syslog records (without packet payloads). The plan specifies UK-region cloud hosting, encryption in transit and at rest, tokenised site and asset identifiers, and role-based access. Source metadata (presence, access and machine metadata) is retained for 90 days on Essential and 12 months on Professional and Assured, whereas incident narratives, actions taken and monthly evidence packs are retained for 36 months. After a contract ends, customer data is scheduled to be returned or deleted within 90 days.
Each month CPSL exports a dated record of incidents detected, plain-English narratives, physical and digital actions taken, and source coverage over the period. This gives a site manager, insurer, auditor or prime contractor clear, consistent operational records to support security questionnaires, insurance reviews and regulatory expectations—though it does not guarantee legal compliance, tender acceptance or formal certification on its own.
